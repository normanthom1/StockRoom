Setting up

Start with the paperwork you already have.

Typing in a stock list is the bit that stops most practices before they start. So don't. Upload a folder of invoices or receipts, photos, PDFs or CSVs, and StockRoom reads them into a draft of what you order, who you buy it from, what you pay and how much you get at a time.

Read down the draft, fix anything it has misread, untick what you don't keep on the shelf, and that's your stock list built. Nothing is added until you say so.

  • Products you buy more than once are ticked for you; one-offs are folded away.
  • Suppliers and prices come off the invoices, so there's no separate setup step.
  • Upload the same invoice twice and the repeat is skipped, not counted again.
{% endif %}

How it works

  1. 01

    Assistants tap what they use

    The buttons are "Used one", "Used the last one" and "Running low". Logging something takes a tap or two between patients, and it still works when the Wi-Fi drops out.

  2. 02

    StockRoom does the maths

    It averages each item's use over the last eight weeks and allows for how long your supplier takes to deliver. It shows its working, so you can see why something says "Order now".

  3. 03

    You order from one list

    Everything that needs ordering sits on one list, sorted by supplier. Ring or email them from there (StockRoom writes the email), then mark it ordered.

    Upload the invoice when the delivery lands. It ticks off what arrived and says "3 of 5 came, 2 still coming" for the rest, updates the prices, moves the runout dates, and keeps the file as your tax record.

Ask StockRoom

Ask "what's running low?" or "what did we spend this month?" in plain English and get an answer from your own stock. Assistants get the how-to answers without the prices.

GST and tax records

What you spent on stock, by New Zealand tax year, with GST split out and a CSV for your accountant. The invoice file itself is kept for seven years, which is what the Tax Administration Act asks for.

A code each

The practice has one login that every device signs in with. After that each person taps their own code, 2 digits for an assistant and 4 for a manager, so you know who logged what without anyone stopping to sign in properly.

Works on the shelf

Install it on the phone or tablet like an app. Big buttons, made for gloves, and taps still log when the Wi-Fi drops out in the back room. They sync when it comes back.

A shared NZ catalogue

A ready-made list of what New Zealand practices order and who sells it, so you can pick your stock rather than type it. Each item keeps a backup supplier for when the usual one is out.

Spending

What you've spent this week, month or year, broken down by supplier, with the items costing you most. Enough to notice a price rise without going through the filing cabinet.

What it changes for a practice manager

  • You hear about the gloves running low while there's still time to get more in.
  • You count less. There's a full stocktake for when you want one, but day to day the numbers come from the people using the stock.
    • {% if ai_enabled %}
  • Invoices stop being a filing job. The one document does the receiving, updates your prices and becomes the record you have to keep anyway.
    • {% endif %}
  • You get a rough idea of spend: what you've ordered this week, month or year, by supplier, without digging through invoices.
  • The practice has one login. Each device signs in once, then each person taps their own code, and assistants never see prices.
  • Getting started is {% if ai_enabled %}a folder of invoices, a look down the draft, and one count of the shelf{% else %}picking your stock from the catalogue and counting it once{% endif %}. There's a setup list that ticks itself off as you go.

What it doesn't do

  • It doesn't place orders. You still ring or email your supplier. StockRoom tells you what to order and when.
  • It doesn't connect to your suppliers' systems or to your practice management software.
  • The forecast isn't much use for the first few weeks. Until it has enough taps and counts to go on, it shows a range instead of a date.
  • It isn't your accounting system. It covers the stock invoices you put through it, not your whole set of expenses, and it doesn't work out what's deductible.
  • It keeps your stock, your suppliers, your staff's names{% if ai_enabled %} and the invoice files you upload{% endif %}. Nothing about patients.
    • {% if ai_enabled %}
  • Photos and PDFs are read by Google Gemini, and it does misread things. That's why you check the preview before anything is saved.
    • {% endif %}
